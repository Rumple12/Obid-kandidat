\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

\subsection[Direct Answers to the Research Questions]{Direct Answers to the\\Research Questions}\label{subsec:conclusions-rqs}

The formal wording of RQ1--RQ3 is retained in
Section~\ref{subsec:intro-rqs}. The following answers summarize the frozen
results and their interpretation.

\paragraph{RQ1.}
Within the frozen cases, \texttt{CONFIG-OBID} produced the expected observable
outcome in all 35 RQ1 attempts. Each of the seven normal, malformed, and
state-dependent families was correct in 5/5 attempts, and modal agreement was
100\% in every family. The extended decision layer was therefore accurate and
consistent across the observed repetitions, including the inclusive threshold,
malformed-input termination, state changes, and duplicate-action suppression.
This answer is limited to the defined cases and five repetitions per family; it
does not establish universal or production reliability.

\paragraph{RQ2.}
In the exercised trials, the runtime controls prevented invalid or unapproved
actions from crossing the frozen action interface. All five injected invalid
actions were
blocked, and all ten pending HITL trials retained the held action without
release. Assigned approvals were correct in 5/5 trials and assigned denials in
4/5. In denial R03, the assigned decision was \texttt{deny} but the submitted
decision was \texttt{approve}. The action remained unreleased with endpoints
0/0 and the fan off while pending, then the unchanged valid action reached
\texttt{/fan/on} once after approval. The run remains incorrect against the
assigned denial oracle and is retained as the single protocol deviation; it was
not an unapproved release. The historical crossing flag of
one is preserved, while the append-only correction controls the final result of
zero observed invalid or unapproved crossings.

\paragraph{RQ3.}
Both configurations handled High, Low, Threshold, Memory A, and Memory C
correctly. The observed reliability differences were confined to Malformed and
Memory B, where \texttt{CONFIG-BASELINE} was 0/5 and \texttt{CONFIG-OBID} was
5/5 in each family. The Obid configuration also had higher median automated
latency in all three measured latency families, with human waiting excluded.
These are descriptive differences between complete configurations; they do not
support statistical significance or attribution to any individual component.

\subsection{Final Contribution}\label{subsec:conclusions-contribution}

The final contribution is a bounded n8n-based agentic decision and reliability
layer that extends the inherited Yacoub-compatible workflow-to-action boundary.
The \texttt{OBID\_CREATED} work comprises one stronger single-agent
configuration with two read-only tools, bounded process-local memory, and
structured internal decisions; contract-specific runtime validation,
deterministic policy, and executable HITL; and a controlled repeated evaluation
with raw-data retention, deterministic processing, and traceability. The
infrastructure, middleware/API, baseline, threshold semantics, and earlier
action-side evidence are inherited from Yacoub; their provenance remains
\texttt{YACOUB\_INHERITED}. The sensor and action contracts and compatible
endpoint meanings are shared; their provenance remains
\texttt{SHARED\_INTERFACE}.
Integration and new measurements do not transfer authorship of those elements.

\subsection{What Was Achieved}\label{subsec:conclusions-achieved}

The work preserved compatibility with the inherited action boundary and added a
working single-agent decision path. It established executable validation,
policy, and HITL release control. It also demonstrated bounded state-aware
behavior through a frozen comparison with the inherited minimal-agent baseline.
Retaining every attempt, processing results deterministically, and linking
summaries to execution evidence made the claims traceable within the study
boundary.

\subsection{What Was Not Achieved}\label{subsec:conclusions-not-achieved}

The study did not establish production safety, universal reliability, or
statistical generalization. It produced no new physical Obid hardware evidence
and did not compare broad multi-agent behavior, a validator agent, alternative
models or memory strategies, multiple device targets, or scalability. The
process-local memory was not evaluated as restart-durable, the latency data did
not isolate component costs, and unavailable direct cost telemetry prevents an
economic conclusion.

\subsection{Future Work}\label{subsec:conclusions-future}

The most important next step is to repeat the evaluation with more attempts,
more negative cases, and runs on separate dates. Further studies
could add instrumented physical hardware and more device or action targets;
strengthen the release path through authentication, timeouts, replay protection,
idempotency, and action integrity; and compare declared model or bounded-memory
configurations under common controls. The optional validator-agent comparison
was not implemented. Its status remains
\texttt{DEFERRED\_AFTER\_FINAL\_FREEZE}. A
separate human-factors study would be required to evaluate HITL comprehension,
workload, decision quality, and response behavior. Each direction would require
new implementation or protocol work, new retained evidence, and an explicit
reopening of the freeze.
