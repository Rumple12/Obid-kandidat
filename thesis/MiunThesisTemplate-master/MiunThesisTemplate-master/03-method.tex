\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

\setlength{\emergencystretch}{4em}

This chapter explains how the study was designed and conducted. It defines the
comparison, the pre-observation oracle, the repeated-run controls, and the
rules used to turn retained execution evidence into measurements for RQ1--RQ3.
The focus is methodological: Chapter~4 explains why the final architecture was
selected, Chapter~5 describes its implementation, and Chapter~6 reports what
was observed.

\subsection{Research and Design Approach}\label{subsec:method-approach}

The study followed a bounded engineering design-and-evaluation approach. A
small inherited workflow-to-action system was extended with one agentic
decision and reliability configuration, and the completed configurations were
then compared under a frozen protocol. The engineering work and the evaluation
were connected, but they had different evidence gates. Source files and
one-off readiness executions could establish that a component existed and was
ready to evaluate; claims about repeated behaviour required the separately
locked experimental records.

The work was organized into the methodological phases in
Table~\ref{tab:method-phases}. This organization avoids treating repository
milestones as the research method itself. In particular, implementation was
completed against an oracle defined earlier, and evaluated semantics were not
changed after repeated observation began.

The three research questions were operationalized once and retained in their
final wording:

\begin{quote}
\noindent\textbf{RQ1.} How accurately and consistently does the extended
n8n-based agentic decision layer produce the expected IoT action across defined
normal, malformed, and state-dependent test cases?

\noindent\textbf{RQ2.} How effectively do runtime structured-output validation,
action policies, and Human-in-the-Loop approval prevent invalid or risky agent
actions from reaching the shared IoT action interface?

\noindent\textbf{RQ3.} What reliability and latency differences are observed
between the inherited minimal agent baseline and the extended Obid agentic
workflow under the same defined IoT test cases?
\end{quote}

Sections~\ref{subsec:method-rq1}--\ref{subsec:method-latency} define the
corresponding measures without reporting their values.

Development and report preparation used AI-assisted repository tooling under
bounded task instructions. The researcher selected the scope, approved key
decisions, performed the necessary private runtime actions, and supplied the
actual HITL decisions. Separate runner, read-only audit, and bounded-repair
roles limited each task and supported review by an independent context. Report
writing was separated from implementation work. Generated code or prose was
not accepted as proof of runtime behaviour: implementation claims required
versioned artifacts and runtime evidence, while experimental claims required
the frozen raw records. Audit passes were quality gates on completed work, not
additional trials or data points.

\subsection{Controlled Temperature-to-Fan Scenario}\label{subsec:method-scenario}

The experiment used one temperature-to-fan scenario as a controlled
event--state--action proxy. A contract-valid sensor event contained exactly
\texttt{sensor\_id}, \texttt{timestamp}, \texttt{type}, \texttt{value}, and
\texttt{unit}. The frozen domain constants were
\texttt{type = temperature} and \texttt{unit = C}. The inherited decision
semantics were inclusive at the boundary:
\begin{quote}
\centering
\texttt{value >= 30.0 C} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{fan\_on},\qquad
\texttt{value < 30.0 C} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{fan\_off}.
\end{quote}

A shared action contained the four fields \texttt{action\_id},
\texttt{target}, \texttt{reason}, and \texttt{requires\_approval}. The only
contract-valid actions were \texttt{fan\_on} and \texttt{fan\_off}, and the
only target was \texttt{fan\_1}. The inherited observable boundary exposed
\texttt{GET /status}, \texttt{POST /sensor-event},
\texttt{POST /fan/on}, and \texttt{POST /fan/off}. The workflows selected the
appropriate fan route.

The evaluated state was a simulated fan in the frozen Yacoub middleware.
Ordinary Step~10 stimuli entered the applicable configuration workflow, while
the malformed case used a direct decision-layer seam described in
Section~\ref{subsec:method-cases}. The experiment did not deploy a new Obid
Raspberry Pi, GPIO connection, or physical fan. The deliberately small
scenario made the event, current state, proposed action, endpoint reach, and
final state observable under controlled conditions. It does not represent all
IoT domains, devices, or risk classes.

\subsection{Frozen Evaluation Protocol}\label{subsec:method-protocol}

The evaluation was governed by
\texttt{OBID-EVALUATION-PROTOCOL-V1}. Its machine-readable oracle was
\codepath{evaluation/cases/obid-evaluation-cases.json}, identified as
\texttt{OBID-EVALUATION-CASES-V1}. Before repeated execution, configuration,
runtime, model, workflow, case, protocol, and contract identities were recorded
in {\small\texttt{STEP10\_EXPERIMENT\_FREEZE\_V1}}. After execution and integrity
checking, the primary records were closed under
{\small\texttt{STEP10\_RAW\_DATA\_LOCK\_V1}}.

The authority order was:
\begin{quote}
\centering
\small
case/oracle freeze $\rightarrow$ implementation readiness $\rightarrow$
experiment freeze $\rightarrow$ repeated execution $\rightarrow$ raw-data
lock $\rightarrow$ deterministic processing $\rightarrow$ reportable
summaries.
\end{quote}

The shared contracts and case/oracle definition were frozen before baseline
reproduction and Obid implementation. The later experiment freeze did not
redefine correctness; it bound the completed implementation and runtime to the
already frozen method. Case expectations, applicability, repetition counts,
injection points, reset rules, metrics, and timing boundaries were therefore
fixed before Step~10 observation. Once repetition~1 began, those evaluated
semantics could not change. Necessary runtime restorations were allowed only
before that point and had to be logged as non-semantic, without replacing a
result. Earlier one-off executions were used to establish readiness; they were
not counted as repeated trials. The protocol thus separated construction
checks, experimental attempts, and later audit activity.

\subsection{Repeated-Run Design and Order Control}\label{subsec:method-repetition}

Five repetitions were planned for every applicable automated
case--configuration cell, five complete A--B--C sequences for each core
configuration, five invalid-action trials, and five trials of each HITL
variant. Five repetitions permit a small descriptive assessment of repeated
sameness while keeping the manually controlled experiment bounded; they do
not support inferential or population-level claims.

The core case blocks were H (High), L (Low), T (Threshold), M (Malformed), and
S (one complete A--B--C sequence). Table~\ref{tab:method-order} shows the
deterministic rotation. Each block occupied every ordinal position once. This
was order balancing, not randomization.

Within a block, the two configurations ran consecutively. The fixed block
indices were H=1, L=2, T=3, M=4, and S=5. For block index $i$ and repetition
$r$, an even $i+r$ used
\texttt{CONFIG-BASELINE} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{CONFIG-OBID}; an odd $i+r$
used the reverse order. This alternated which configuration received the
earlier temporal position. For S, the first configuration completed A, B, and
C before the paired configuration began; records were never alternated inside
the sequence.

Independent cases began by restoring the required simulated fan state,
resetting configuration state or memory where applicable, recording the
precondition, and then injecting the stimulus. Unrelated prior runs were not
allowed to influence the case. Independent CONFIG-OBID cases received fresh
synthetic session identifiers. Within S, state was preserved between A, B,
and C, and CONFIG-OBID used one synthetic session identifier across those
three interactions. After each complete sequence, physical and configuration
state were reset before the other configuration's complete sequence and before
the next repetition. CONFIG-BASELINE had no memory state to reset, and its
lack of memory was not itself scored as a failure.

Each S execution contributed three primary records, whereas H, L, T, and M
each contributed one. Across two configurations and five rounds this produced
the planned 70-record core denominator. After all core rounds, the five
invalid-action trials ran first. HITL trials
then used approval--denial in rounds 1, 3, and 5, and denial--approval in
rounds 2 and 4. No safety-only case interrupted the comparison. The planned
experimental denominator was therefore 70 core records, five invalid-action
records, and ten HITL records: 85 primary records in total. Chapter~6 reports
actual execution, not this section.

\subsection[Correctness and Consistency Measurement]{Correctness and
Consistency\texorpdfstring{\newline}{ }Measurement}\label{subsec:method-rq1}

Correctness was evaluated per attempted run against the frozen observable
oracle. For an action-producing case, a correct attempt required the expected
terminal, matching \codepath{action\_id}, \texttt{target}, and
\codepath{requires\_approval}, a non-empty schema-valid reason, endpoint reach
exactly once at the matching route when release was expected, no other endpoint
request, and the expected final simulated state.
Exact natural-language reason wording was not scored. For a no-action case,
any shared action or fan-endpoint request made the attempt incorrect.

Failures, errors, timeouts, rejects, missing observations, and unexpected
terminals remained in the attempted denominator:
\[
\text{correctness percentage}
=\frac{\text{correct attempts}}{\text{total attempted attempts}}\times100.
\]

Consistency was measured as modal observable-outcome agreement. Let $m$ be the
largest count of one normalized observable outcome signature and $n$ the total
number of attempted attempts in the family:
\[
\text{modal agreement percentage}
=\frac{m}{n}\times100.
\]
The normalized signature used only observable fields: run status, terminal
stage, shared-action presence, contractual action values (with reason reduced
to validity rather than exact phrasing), endpoint reach, and final state. A
high modal value means repeated sameness; it does not imply agreement with the
oracle. The measures were grouped by case family across the five attempts.

the three research questions.

\subsection{Safety and HITL Measurement}\label{subsec:method-rq2}

RQ2 treated interface crossing as the primary safety observation. Each
applicable record retained the injected or proposed candidate, validator and
policy outcomes, approval requirement, held and released actions, actual human
decision, shared-interface and endpoint reach, terminal stage, simulated
state, and status/error. The invalid-action trial asked whether the known
contract-invalid candidate could pass the real validator. HITL trials asked
whether an action remained held while unapproved and whether later release
matched the human decision.

Before human input, a separate pending snapshot recorded a waiting execution,
the held action, null release, both fan-endpoint counts, and the simulated fan
state. The orchestration did not submit the human decision automatically. The
researcher supplied \texttt{approve} or \texttt{deny} to the actual waiting
form, after which the released action and terminal were observed. Approval
could authorize release only of the unchanged held four-field action; denial
was expected to release nothing.

The assigned trial stimulus and the submitted human decision were treated as
separate variables. If they differed, assigned-case correctness was scored
against the frozen assignment, while runtime release safety was assessed
against the decision actually submitted. The mismatch had to remain a
protocol deviation. If a locked derived interpretation later required
correction, the method required an append-only record naming the affected
run and preserving the original raw and processed artifacts. The run could
not be repeated or rewritten. Chapter~6 shows how this distinction was applied
to the observations.

\subsection{Automated Latency Measurement}\label{subsec:method-latency}

The comparable RQ3 latency subset was fixed to High, Low, and Threshold under
both configurations: three cases times five repetitions times two
configurations, or 30 planned observations. Malformed, memory, invalid-action,
and HITL cases were excluded from this automated comparison.

Timing began when the selected configuration ingress received the stimulus and
started processing. It ended when the final automated terminal completed,
including receipt of the inherited fan-endpoint response for an action-producing
run. Each cell retained five planned duration slots containing either the raw
duration or an explicit missing value. Primary summaries used the available
retained durations, with completeness reported alongside them. The summaries
were median, minimum, and maximum; the arithmetic mean could be included only
as a supplementary description. Missing timing remained missing, and no
outlier was removed.

No significance test, p-value, confidence interval, or component-level causal
attribution was planned. The configurations performed different complete
workloads, so the measurement compared end-to-end configurations. HITL human
waiting was excluded. Where present, pre-wait automated processing, human
waiting, post-decision automated processing, and total elapsed time were stored
separately and were not used to make population claims about human response.

\subsection[Raw-Data Retention and Failure Handling]{Raw-Data Retention and
\texorpdfstring{\newline}{ }Failure Handling}\label{subsec:method-raw}

Every attempted repetition, identified by a stable primary run ID, remained in
the denominator. A failed run
could not be replaced by a successful rerun under the same identity, and
errors, timeouts, rejects, missing values, and unexpected terminals were
retained. Attempt-start and result events made a missing or interrupted
attempt distinguishable from a run that was never scheduled.

The raw evidence set retained the planned order, actual order, attempt events,
one primary run record per attempted repetition, HITL pending snapshots,
operational deviations, execution IDs, timestamps where available, errors,
and missing observations. A stable primary run ID represented the experimental
repetition. A top-level n8n execution ID linked that identity to runtime
evidence; child execution IDs documented component calls but were not counted
as additional repetitions.

After execution, the raw manifest checked file hashes, parseability, expected
group sizes, identity uniqueness, schedule linkage, start/result pairing,
absence of replacement, pending-snapshot linkage, and a bounded privacy scan.
Only then was the raw lock declared. Processing did not replace this authority:
the hierarchy was raw records $\rightarrow$ processed summaries
$\rightarrow$ report claims. Operational deviations remained in their own
append-only record so infrastructure restoration could not be confused with
an evaluated semantic change.

\subsection[Processing, Traceability, and Reproducibility]{Processing,
Traceability, and\texorpdfstring{\newline}{ }
Reproducibility}\label{subsec:method-processing}

Three programs had separate methodological roles. The orchestration program
enforced the frozen schedule, verified configuration identities, controlled
resets and preconditions, invoked the fixed configurations or seams, assigned
stable run IDs, and appended attempt evidence. It did not change decision
semantics, retry a primary attempt, or choose HITL decisions for the human.

The extractor read a bounded, privacy-scoped projection from the retained n8n
database in read-only mode. It exposed allowlisted execution status, node
output, timing, model/tool/memory observations, safe error fields, and
parent--child lineage needed by the protocol. During freeze checks it also
read bounded workflow and webhook metadata. Credential-bearing workflow data,
private account information, form-resume URLs, and secrets were excluded or
redacted. The extractor neither requested hidden chain-of-thought nor inferred
unrecorded model processes.

The processing program first validated the raw lock and re-hashed its inputs.
It then computed the frozen correctness and modal-agreement summaries,
descriptive latency, separate HITL timing, and traceability outputs. Failures
and missing values were retained; the program did not remove outliers, fill
missing evidence, or estimate unavailable cost. A processed manifest recorded
the resulting output identities.

Traceability followed:
\begin{quote}
\centering
\small
reported summary $\rightarrow$ primary run ID $\rightarrow$ top-level n8n
execution ID $\rightarrow$ raw evidence.
\end{quote}
Linked child executions supported the final arrow without increasing the
repetition count.

Reproduction controls included n8n \texttt{1.123.37} and its pinned container
digest, the frozen model identifier and stored empty options, no fallback,
versioned workflow exports, prompts, tools, memory and contracts, protocol and
case hashes, the experiment freeze, raw and processed manifests, and the final
artifact index. Credential values were never committed, and failures and
deviations remained visible. Direct cost telemetry was unavailable and was not
estimated. After processing and the append-only correction mechanism were in
place, \texttt{OBID\_FINAL\_ARTIFACT\_FREEZE\_V1} indexed the frozen
reportable package; later report writing did not reopen that substantive
freeze. These controls support artifact and processing
reproduction, not perfect recreation of every external condition. The remote
provider backend and unstored defaults may change, a private model credential
must be attached locally, and process-local memory is not durable across a
restart.

The observable oracle is intentionally narrow: it establishes whether the
defined action boundary behaved as expected for the frozen cases, not whether
the system is universally reliable or production-safe. Controlled injection
allows the validator and HITL gate to be tested at exact stages, but it does
not reproduce every route a naturally generated error might take. Similarly,
the two-interaction memory is one process-local strategy, and the study does
not compare it with longer, persistent, or alternative memory designs.

The planned five attempts per cell support transparent descriptive summaries,
not statistical generalization. Human decisions and waiting times belong to
one controlled execution context. Provider-side implementation, private
credential attachment, and some default generation behaviour remain outside
the frozen repository package. Finally, the simulated fan makes state changes
observable without conflating the study with hardware deployment, but it
precludes claims about new physical Obid operation. These limitations bound
the conclusions to the two frozen configurations, the stated scenario, and
the retained evidence chain.
