\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\setlength{\emergencystretch}{4em}

This chapter presents the observations from the frozen repeated evaluation. It
reports execution integrity before turning to the three research questions,
automated latency, HITL timing, telemetry availability, and retained negative
outcomes. The chapter distinguishes assigned test outcomes from runtime
observations, particularly for the third assigned-denial trial. Explanations
of why the configurations differed, the implications of those differences,
and threats to validity are deferred to Chapter~7.

\subsection{Evaluation Overview}\label{subsec:results-overview}

The evaluation compared exactly two configurations at the same inherited
temperature-to-fan boundary. \texttt{CONFIG-BASELINE} was the stateless minimal
agent inherited from Yacoub and is therefore labelled
\texttt{YACOUB\_INHERITED}. \texttt{CONFIG-OBID} was the final Obid-created
single-agent workflow with bounded memory, validation, policy, and HITL. Both
used \texttt{models/gemini-2.5-flash}, stored generation options
\texttt{\{\}}, and no fallback model. No validator-agent or multi-agent
configuration formed part of the experiment. The action-side boundary was the
frozen Yacoub middleware with a simulated fan; the results do not represent a
new physical Obid hardware test.

The results address the research questions in their frozen wording:

\noindent\textbf{RQ1.} How accurately and consistently does the extended n8n-based
agentic decision layer produce the expected IoT action across defined normal,
malformed, and state-dependent test cases?

\noindent\textbf{RQ2.} How effectively do runtime structured-output validation, action
policies, and Human-in-the-Loop approval prevent invalid or risky agent actions
from reaching the shared IoT action interface?

\noindent\textbf{RQ3.} What reliability and latency differences are observed between the
inherited minimal agent baseline and the extended Obid agentic workflow under
the same defined IoT test cases?

The recorded dataset contains 70 core comparison attempts, five controlled
invalid-action attempts, and ten HITL attempts. The core comparison supplies
the RQ1 observations for \texttt{CONFIG-OBID} and the paired RQ3 reliability
observations. Thirty of the core attempts---High, Low, and Threshold under both
configurations---form the automated-latency subset. The invalid-action and HITL
phases supply the RQ2 observations. HITL human-wait time is reported separately
and is not included in the automated-latency subset.

The pre-observation case oracle and protocol define correctness. Locked raw
Step~10 records control what was observed. For the one R03 interpretation
issue, the append-only correction is applied above the historical processed
classification without changing either raw or processed bytes. Processed
tables and traceability then provide reproducible summaries linked to the raw
run and top-level execution identities. Development and readiness evidence
from earlier steps is used only in Section~\ref{subsec:results-negative} and is
not substituted for a Step~10 result.

\subsection[RQ2 - Invalid-Action Validation Results]{RQ2 - Invalid-Action
\texorpdfstring{\newline}{ }Validation Results}\label{subsec:results-rq2-invalid}

The invalid-action phase submitted the unsupported candidate
\codepath{fan\_reverse} five times through a controlled fault-injection seam.
The injection occurred after the agent and before the runtime validator; the
candidate was not generated naturally by the model during the repeated
experiment. All five attempts ended at validation with reason code
\codepath{UNKNOWN\_ACTION}. The candidate was invalid against the frozen action
schema and the recorded terminal stage was the runtime validator. Policy
release was not reached, no shared action was emitted, both endpoint counts
remained zero, and the simulated fan remained off. The five raw records and
their attempt events showed the same terminal pattern.

Thus, all five injected \codepath{fan\_reverse} candidates were blocked at the
runtime validator before policy release or an action endpoint was reached.
This observation concerns the one frozen unsupported-action form at the stated
injection point. It does not show that every malformed or adversarial input
would be blocked.

\subsection{Summary of Results}\label{subsec:results-summary}

The frozen evaluation retained all 85 scheduled primary attempts in their
planned order, with 85 unique run IDs and 85 unique top-level execution IDs.
No repetition was replaced. Within the seven frozen RQ1 families,
\texttt{CONFIG-OBID} produced the expected observable outcome in 35/35
attempts and had 100\% modal agreement in every family.

For RQ2, all five injected \texttt{fan\_reverse} candidates were blocked, and
all ten HITL actions remained unreleased while pending. Assigned approvals
were correct in 5/5 trials. Assigned denials were correct in 4/5 because R03
received actual approval rather than the assigned denial. The final account
therefore records five planned approvals and five planned denials, six actual
approvals and four actual denials, one protocol deviation, and zero invalid or
unapproved crossings while retaining the historical processed crossing flag.

For RQ3, the configurations produced the same 5/5 correctness in High, Low,
Threshold, Memory A, and Memory C. They differed in Malformed and Memory B,
where the baseline recorded 0/5 and the Obid configuration recorded 5/5. Across
the 30 automated-latency observations, the Obid configuration had the higher
median in all three measured families. HITL timing remained separate, and
missing token and cost telemetry was left unavailable rather than estimated.
Chapter~7 considers their interpretation and limitations.
